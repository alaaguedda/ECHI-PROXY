Multi-microphone · assistive listening

Can a pair of smart glasses help you hear the conversation?

A small, fully reproducible study on open data: four microphones on a glasses frame, a wearer who is much louder than everyone else, two conversation partners and real background noise.

Source code ↗

By Alaa Guedda · inspired by the ECHI-2 conversation-enhancement task

The problem

People with hearing loss lose the cocktail party.

Assistive listening should lift everyone in the conversation, remove the noise, the competing talkers and the wearer's own voice, and do it fast enough to keep lips and sound in sync.

The real challenge data comes from smart-glasses recordings. Here I build an open stand-in with the same ingredients and ask three focused questions.

RQ1
Do more microphones help?

Same network, 1 vs 2 vs 4 microphones. Does spatial information change what is possible?

RQ2
What does low latency cost?

Causal processing (32 ms) against a model that sees the whole segment.

RQ3
Where does it break?

Wearer loudness, noise level, reverberation and noise type.

Takeaways

What this shows.

Honest limits

Simulated array and rooms. The noise comes from a different array, so it is spatially coherent (inter-channel correlation about 0.87 to 0.90). One training run per model, mask applied to one reference microphone, no binaural rendering.

Next, with the real glasses data

Binaural rendering, spatially selective deep filters, stronger backbones such as TF-GridNet, hybrid model-based plus neural separation, and the multimodal track with video and motion sensors.
